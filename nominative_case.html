Хто? Що?

Who? What?

Називний відмінок

The Nominative case (Називний відмінок) answers Хто? (who?) and Що? (what?). It's the dictionary form: the version of a word you'd look up, with no ending changes of its own. Every other case is defined by how it changes away from this one, so getting comfortable with Nominative first, especially recognising a noun's gender on sight, is what makes every later case click into place. Nominative is used in four situations:

  • Subject is the noun doing the action or being described: Студент читає (The student reads).
  • Numerals 1 and 2–4 keep the counted noun singular for one, nominative plural for two to four: один рік, два квитки.
  • Comparison with «як» (like/as) leaves the compared noun nominative: як Україна (like Ukraine).
  • Predicate after «є» (is), which is most often omitted, appears in sentences like Київ — чудове місто (Kyiv is a great city).

Because nothing changes here, the 26 practice exercises below focus on what actually matters at this stage: recognising gender by ending, forming the plural, and spotting when a word that looks nominative isn't, because something (немає, a preposition, a direct object) has overridden it.

The -ь trap: unlike every other ending, a final -ь gives you no clue at all whether a noun is masculine or feminine. кінь (horse) and день (day) are masculine; сіль (salt) and ніч (night) are feminine, with nothing in the spelling to tell them apart. These have to be memorised one at a time, ideally alongside an adjective (цей кінь vs ця сіль) so the gender sticks.

Exceptions by meaning: a handful of words are masculine because they refer to a male person, regardless of their ending: батько (father), тато (dad), дядько (uncle), and male names like Микола, Ілля, Петро. A small group of "common-gender" profession nouns (суддя, meaning judge, and колега, meaning colleague) default to masculine unless context specifies a woman.

Plural endings by gender

MasculineFeminineNeuter
GenderTypical plural endingExample
Masculine-и (hard stem)студент → студенти
Masculine-і / -ї (soft stem: й, ь, ж, ч, ш, щ)музей → музеї, вчитель → вчителі
Feminine-и (hard -а stem)мама → мами
Feminine-і (soft -я, -ь stem)земля → землі, сіль → солі
Neuter-а (hard -о stem)вікно → вікна
Neuter-я (soft -е stem)море → моря

Abstract -ття/-ння/-лля neuter nouns (життя, знання, весілля, взуття) usually stay unchanged in the plural, the same way they stay unchanged in the genitive.

Vowel alternations carry over from what you've seen elsewhere: стіл → столи (і↔о), сіль → солі, ніч → ночі, річ → речі, and fleeting vowels like день → дні, замок → замки.

Irregular plurals to memorise: людина → люди, дитина → діти, друг → друзі, ім'я → імена, око → очі, мати → матері.

Numerals in more detail

один/одна/одне (one) takes a singular noun. два/три/чотири (2–4) take the nominative plural. From five upwards, and with «скільки?» (how many?), the noun switches to the genitive plural instead (п'ять студентів); that pattern isn't drilled here, since it belongs to the Genitive case.
