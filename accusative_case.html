Кого? Що? Куди? Коли?

Whom? What? Where to? When?

Знахідний відмінок

The Accusative case (Знахідний відмінок) answers Кого? (whom?), Що? (what?), Куди? (where to?) and Коли? (when?). Its main job is marking the direct object: here's what that means.

Оксанаsubject
вітаєverb
→
Тарасаobject

Oksana congratulates Taras.

Оксана is doing the congratulating; she's the subject, so she stays nominative. Тарас is the one it's done to; he's the direct object, which is why he changes: Тарас → Тараса. It's called direct because nothing stands between the verb and him: no preposition, no middleman.

That's the core job. But Accusative does more than just objects; there are five main triggers:

  • Direct object is the thing or person a verb acts on, as above.
  • Direction of motion answers куди? (where to?), after motion verbs with в/на: Я йду в кіно (I'm going to the cinema).
  • Days of the week answer коли? (when?): У середу буде урок (The lesson will be on Wednesday).
  • Sports & games answer у що?: Діти грають у футбол (Kids play football).
  • Verbs paired with a preposition, such as чекати на, сподіватися на, дивитися на, and відповідати на, take the accusative.

The Animacy Rule only affects masculine nouns. An animate masculine noun (a person or animal) takes the same form as the genitive in the accusative; an inanimate masculine noun stays identical to the nominative. Feminine and neuter nouns don't show this split; they have one accusative form regardless of animacy. The Animacy Rule, explained

This is the defining feature of the accusative case. It applies only to masculine nouns (feminine and neuter nouns already have a single accusative form regardless of what they refer to):

  • Animate masculine nouns (people, animals) → accusative = genitive form: бачу брата, бачу кота.
  • Inanimate masculine nouns (objects, places) → accusative = nominative form: бачу стіл, бачу дім.

Plural (all genders)

Animate plural nouns take the genitive plural form, and inanimate plural nouns take the nominative plural form: бачу студентів (animate) vs. бачу книги (inanimate).

Edge cases exist where Ukrainian's grammatical animacy doesn't match biological reality: a corpse (мрець) is grammatically animate, a virus (вірус) is grammatically inanimate, and collective nouns for groups of people (народ, клас, натовп) are grammatically inanimate even though they refer to people.

Direction vs. location: на / у

The same prepositions на and у can trigger either the accusative or the locative, depending on the question being answered:

QuestionCaseExample
Де? (Where, location)LocativeЯ сиджу в автобусі. (I am sitting on the bus.)
Куди? (Where to, direction)AccusativeЯ сідаю в автобус. (I am getting on the bus.)

Note: "до" (to) always takes the genitive, not the accusative, even though it also answers куди?: Я лечу до України.

Verbs that take the accusative

  • Perception: бачити (to see), чути (to hear), знати (to know), пам'ятати (to remember)
  • Feeling/emotion: любити (to love), ненавидіти (to hate), поважати (to respect), шкодувати (to regret)
  • Action with object: мати (to have), купувати (to buy), нести/брати (to carry/take), будувати (to build)
  • Verb + preposition: чекати на (to wait for), сподіватися на (to hope for), дивитися на (to look at), відповідати на (to answer)

A fixed idiom worth knowing

"Мене звати..." (My name is..., literally "They call me...") always uses the accusative pronoun мене, regardless of who's speaking: Мене звати Оксана. Його звати Тарас.
