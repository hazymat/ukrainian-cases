# Ukrainian Cases: Practice

Free, interactive, and gamified practice pages for English-speaking learners of Ukrainian, working through
the grammatical case system one case at a time. Each case comes with its own visual study guide explaining
the rules, plus exercises built to drill case usage, word endings, and distinguishing between different cases.

No sign-up, no tracking, no backend; everything runs in your browser and your progress is saved locally on
your own device. A family-friendly resource for teachers and learners of Ukrainian language.

**Start here: [hazymat.github.io/ukrainian-cases](https://hazymat.github.io/ukrainian-cases/)**

## How the exercises work

Ukrainian's case system is a genuinely unfamiliar concept for most English speakers, so every exercise is
built to keep translation close at hand rather than making you hold two languages in your head at once:

- **Instant, visual feedback.** Tap an answer and it lights up immediately, green for correct, red for
  wrong, with the actual right answer shown either way, so you're never left wondering.
- **A plain-language rule under every question.** Each one names the actual grammar rule at play (possession,
  negation, a specific preposition, a numeral rule, and so on), not just whether you got it right.
- **Ukrainian first, English underneath, everywhere.** Every question, option, and explanation is bilingual,
  Ukrainian upright and prominent, the English gloss in italics below it. The point is to keep translation
  one glance away for anything unfamiliar, rather than letting a single unknown word stall you out.
- **Four exercise types per case:**
  - *Usage recognition*: why does this sentence need this case at all?
  - *Vocabulary & endings*: a two-step question, first confirm what the word means, then pick its correct
    case form, so you're building vocabulary and gender recognition at the same time as the case itself.
  - *Apply the case*: fill in the blank, toggle between typing the answer yourself or picking from options.
  - *Revision*: click the word in the sentence that's in the target case, including deliberate "false
    friend" sentences that look like they need it but don't, to stop you from pattern-matching blindly.
- **Detailed notes**, a collapsible accordion of full rule tables and endings for whenever you want the
  reference material rather than another question.

## A bit of gamification

Finishing an exercise isn't just a checkmark:

- **Themed rewards, every time.** Each score out of 10 draws from a pool of Ukrainian food- and
  culture-themed lines, roast-y below 7, warm and proud above it, so the same exercise doesn't feel the
  same way twice.
- **Confetti for a perfect score.** 10/10 gets a small animated burst instead of the usual completion flash.
- **A trophy shelf.** Every reward earned shows up in a small collection on the case page it came from, and
  in one combined [trophy shelf](https://hazymat.github.io/ukrainian-cases/trophy.html) across every case.
  Tap a trophy to see the line again.
- **Personal bests.** Beating your own previous score on an exercise gets its own "new record" badge.
- **Skipped-question flags.** If you answer out of order and leave a gap, that question is quietly
  highlighted until you go back and fill it in.
- **Reset at any level**: a single exercise, a whole case page, or every case at once, each with its own
  confirmation where it matters.

## About this project

Each page assumes only the Nominative case as prior knowledge, so they can be worked through in any order,
though Genitive and Accusative are traditionally taught first. See [CONVENTIONS.md](CONVENTIONS.md) for the
house style and content conventions used throughout.

Corrections and suggestions welcome via [GitHub Issues](https://github.com/hazymat/ukrainian-cases/issues).
